    Let Blogs Be Blogs Again

    From middle-school Hexo builds, Baota panels, and Sakurairo themes to a blank sheet of native HTML — when style stops being mere decoration, a blog breathes, grows, and evolves with thought again.

    § 01Early Rigs and Quiet Obsessions

    Counting back to middle school, I have been tinkering with personal blogs for quite a few years now.

    It began with Hexo on GitHub Pages01. Then came a sketchy bargain shared host bought off Xianyu — a tiny reseller chopping up an Alibaba Cloud lightweight server behind a Baota control panel, with bandwidth so tight every megabyte hurt02. Later, when Tencent Cloud Lighthouse launched, I finally owned a VPS of my own. I built WordPress on it by hand and tweaked themes tirelessly — to this day, the Sakurairo theme in my memory still feels breathtakingly beautiful03.

    01 Hexo & GitHub Pages: A Node.js static blog generator paired with free GitHub hosting — write Markdown, run hexo g && hexo d, and claim your first teenage plot on the web.
    02 Xianyu Host & Baota Panel: On China's secondhand marketplace Xianyu, micro-resellers sliced single budget cloud instances into tiny slots managed solely via Baota (BT Panel) — a web GUI with no root SSH and razor-thin bandwidth caps.
    03 Lighthouse & Sakurairo: Tencent Cloud's entry-level VPS bundle; Sakurairo (derived from Mashiro's Sakura) became an iconic Chinese WordPress theme beloved for its luminous cover imagery and obsessive polish.

    I even sulked over being under eighteen — too young to pass ICP filing, which meant I couldn't host the site on a Mainland China VPS or accelerate it through Alibaba Cloud DCDN.04

    04 Under-18 ICP Filing & DCDN: Running a public site on Mainland China servers or domestic CDNs (like Alibaba Cloud DCDN) legally requires an ICP registration, which mandates applicants be at least 18 — locking out countless middle- and high-school webmasters.

    I fretted endlessly over which image host could hold my photos and videos. Cloud object storage was too expensive, yet I still wanted a blog nobody visited to greet its very first reader in peak condition.05

    05 Image Hosts (图床) & Object Storage: To avoid steep cloud egress bills on student budgets, early indie bloggers hunted for free third-party image hosts — constantly balancing zero cost against hotlink blocks and sudden shutdowns.

    After all that tinkering, entrance exams and school pressures caught up with me. The blog fell quiet. The server expired, and I didn't renew it. Yet if you asked me today to retrace how I deployed WordPress seven years ago, my hands would still move without hesitation — just as fluently as back then, only noticing far more of the craft in the details.

    § 02Putting Down the Crutch of Frameworks

    Then the wave of large language models swept in, and heavy CMS stacks began to feel redundant — even confining. I moved to lighter frameworks like Astro and VuePress, placing my writing out on the edge nodes of Cloudflare Workers.06

    06 Astro, VuePress & the Edge: Content-first static frameworks deployed across Cloudflare's global edge network, liberating personal blogs from single-VPS renewals and database upkeep.

    And arriving at today, it feels like we can finally set down the crutch of frameworks altogether. AI has collapsed the distance between an idea and its finished form, turning the browser back into a pure creator's canvas: inside a single HTML page, people now build luminous 3D worlds, stage dynamic interactive explainers, and even pack in an entire 3D shooter.

    Three years ago, if a thought like that had crossed my mind, I would have wondered whether I had eaten the wrong wild mushrooms.07

    07 "Eaten the wrong wild mushrooms" (吃错了菌子): A warm Southwest Chinese idiom (especially familiar in Yunnan and Sichuan) referencing hallucinogenic wild mushrooms — humorously asking whether one is daydreaming or seeing things.

    Yet today, with the vivid imagination of Gemini 4 Neon in my left hand and the disciplined execution of GPT 6.2 Astra in my right, a blank sheet of HTML can finally grow into whatever shape lives in my head.

    Everything has changed, except the person behind the keyboard.08

    08 物非人是 (Wù fēi rén shì): A playful inversion of the classical Chinese idiom wù shì rén fēi ("things remain while people change"). The servers, themes, and tools are unrecognizable, yet the curious tinkerer remains the same kid.

    § 03From Infrastructure to Spirit

    Beyond nostalgia, what I feel most is exhilaration. My blog no longer has to live inside cookie-cutter CSDN layouts — or the heirloom CSS snippets passed around in every theme group chat09. Now it can breathe, grow, and evolve alongside my own train of thought.

    09 CSDN & "Heirloom CSS": CSDN is one of China's oldest developer portals, notorious for monotonous templates; meanwhile, indie theme group chats recycled the same copy-pasted CSS tweaks until personal blogs quietly converged into lookalikes.

    Style is no longer mere ornament dressed up to look pretty; it has become a direct conduit for personal sensibility. Moving from worrying about material plumbing to caring about the human spirit itself — that is a quiet leap forward.

    § 04Open Source, Witness, and a New Toy

    So I have open-sourced every single line of code behind this place. Perhaps ten years from now, "independent blogs" will be relics of an older web, and I will have grown into new ways of expressing myself. Even so, the commit history of this repository will preserve one clear record: how, in this era, LLMs changed the way I lived, thought, and created.

    A quiet witness to history, maybe?

    Truthfully, I am probably just a kid who has been handed a brand-new toy — unwrapping it with sheer delight, turning it over in my hands, unable to wait a second longer to hold up the best ideas in my head and show them to the world.
